# 3-minute pitch — script v2

## S1 — 0:00–0:18 · 44 w

A child survived an embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma. His cells are prone to miscounting chromosomes when
they divide — mosaic variegated aneuploidy.

We were asked which existing drug could help him. The honest answer today is none, and the useful
part is why.

## S2 — 0:18–0:42 · 57 w

First the variant. Two independent analyses, required to agree: a hypothesis-free genome-wide run
driven only by his eight clinical terms, and an eleven-gene panel with every coordinate fetched from
a versioned database.

Both land on BUB1B — one nonsense allele, one missense. We cannot prove the two are on opposite
chromosomes. There are no parental samples, so this is a diagnosis pending segregation.

## S3 — 0:42–1:00 · 44 w

We then ran eleven controls. Remove any single symptom and BUB1B still ranks first. But substitute
completely unrelated symptoms — seizures, hearing loss, a heart defect — and it still ranks first.

So the signal comes from the variant, not the phenotype. We report that, because it weakens us.

## S4 — 1:00–1:22 · 52 w

For the drug, five filters instead of the usual two. Approval and a plausible mechanism are
necessary, but they are not enough.

Filter three is pharmacokinetic: does the achievable plasma level reach the concentration that works
in a dish?

Filter five, rarely applied: does the drug make mis-segregation worse?

## S5 — 1:22–1:42 · 48 w

Metformin is the candidate the literature points to, and it dies on filter three. Therapeutic plasma
levels are micromolar; inhibiting complex I needs millimolar — a thousand times higher.

We do not reject metformin out of caution. We reject it with a number.

## S6 — 1:42–2:10 · 66 w

What survives is bortezomib. Aneuploid cells carry a protein imbalance, so they lean on the
proteasome. In myeloma patients, higher aneuploidy tracked with response to proteasome inhibitors.

The concentration reaches: effective below forty nanomolar, against a clinical peak of two hundred
thirty-one to three hundred twelve.

But that is cancer aneuploidy, not constitutional. Our experiment is built to break that transfer.

## S7 — 2:10–2:32 · 52 w

And bortezomib fails filter four for him. Motor neuropathy in eight percent of children, on top of
existing muscle atrophy.

So what actually changes his prognosis today is not a molecule. It is surveillance — three-monthly
abdominal ultrasound and clinical examination, because the realistic threat is a second tumour.

## S8 — 2:32–3:00 · 64 w

If that tumour comes, filter four inverts, and bortezomib becomes a tumour-board hypothesis with a
concentration margin, a stopping rule, and an eight-week experiment that could kill it.

The five-filter framework is the part that scales. It applies to any rare disease where someone is
about to propose an approved drug.

This is not a prescription. It is a prepared, testable answer.
