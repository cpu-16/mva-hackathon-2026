A hypothesis-free genome-wide run driven only by his eight clinical terms, and an eleven-gene panel with coordinates fetched from a versioned database. Both land on BUB1B.

Five filters, not two.

Filters three and five are the ones usually skipped, and they eliminate the most candidates.

Metformin dies on the concentration filter.

Nobody has measured what concentration it needs against aneuploid cells, so it cannot be evaluated on the mechanism proposed. And that mechanism, inhibiting complex I, needs about a thousand times more drug than is safe.

What changes his prognosis today is not a molecule.

Bortezomib fails filter four for him: motor neuropathy in 8% of children, on top of existing muscle atrophy. The realistic threat is a second embryonal tumour — so the intervention that matters now is surveillance.

What this is not
Not a prescription.
The proteasome dependency is shown in cancer aneuploidy. Whether it transfers to constitutional MVA is unproven — which is exactly what the experiment tests.
What this is
A prepared,
testable answer.
A tumour-board hypothesis with a concentration margin, a stopping rule, and an eight-week assay — 12–16 weeks from a fresh biopsy — that could fail to support it.
Two things scale: the five-filter worksheet, for any rare disease where someone is about to propose an approved drug — and MVA-Replay, one file, no new dependencies, --self-check, which another team runs on their own gene in an afternoon.
Five analyses were pre-registered in git before the data was touched. Four of the five came back against our own argument, and all four are in the report.
