## S1 — 0:00–0:15 · 41 w

A child survived an embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma. His cells are prone to miscounting chromosomes when they divide — mosaic variegated aneuploidy. We were asked which existing drug could help him. The honest answer today is none, and the useful part is why.

## S2 — 0:15–0:43 · 63 w

First the variant. Two independent analyses, required to agree: a hypothesis-free genome-wide run driven only by his eight clinical terms, and an eleven-gene panel with every coordinate fetched from a versioned database. Both land on BUB1B — one nonsense allele, one missense. We cannot prove the two are on opposite chromosomes. There are no parental samples, so this is a diagnosis pending segregation.

## S3 — 0:43–1:07 · 58 w

We ran twelve controls. Remove any single symptom and BUB1B still ranks first. Then we caught our own error: two symptoms we called unrelated are features of this disease, so we re-ran with five verified ones, and it still ranked first. The signal is in the variant, not the phenotype, and we report it because it weakens us.

## S4 — 1:07–1:29 · 49 w

For the drug, five filters instead of the usual two. Approval and a plausible mechanism are necessary, but they are not enough. Filter three is pharmacokinetic: does the achievable plasma level reach the concentration that works in a dish? Filter five, rarely applied: does the drug make mis-segregation worse?

## S5 — 1:29–1:48 · 42 w

Metformin is the candidate the literature points to. Nobody has measured what concentration it needs against aneuploid cells, so it cannot be evaluated on the mechanism proposed. And that mechanism, inhibiting complex one, needs a thousand times more drug than is safe.

## S6 — 1:48–2:16 · 61 w

What survives is bortezomib. Aneuploid cells carry a protein imbalance, so they lean on the proteasome. In myeloma patients, higher aneuploidy tracked with response to proteasome inhibitors. The concentration reaches: effective below forty nanomolar, against a clinical peak of two hundred thirty-one to three hundred twelve. But that is cancer aneuploidy, not constitutional. Our experiment is built to break that transfer.

## S7 — 2:16–2:35 · 42 w

And bortezomib fails filter four for him: motor neuropathy in eight percent of children, on existing muscle atrophy. What changes his prognosis today is not a molecule, it is surveillance, and published consensus says renal ultrasound every three months, to age seven.

## S8 — 2:35–2:58 · 55 w

If that tumour comes, the balance may shift, and bortezomib becomes a question for a tumour board, with a concentration margin, a stopping rule, and an experiment that could fail. The five-filter framework is what scales, to any rare disease where someone proposes an approved drug. Not a prescription, but a prepared and testable answer.
